1. INTRODUCTION & CONTEXT
-------------------------
Quantum computers utilizing Shor's Algorithm will be capable of breaking 
classical public-key cryptosystems (RSA, Diffie-Hellman, ECC) in polynomial time.
To mitigate this threat, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
has standardized several Post-Quantum Cryptography algorithms:

- ML-KEM (formerly Kyber): A Key Encapsulation Mechanism based on the Module 
  Learning-with-Errors (M-LWE) problem.
- ML-DSA (formerly Dilithium): A digital signature scheme based on the Module 
  Learning-with-Errors and Short Integer Solution (M-SIS) problems.
- FN-DSA (formerly Falcon): A fast, compact digital signature scheme based on
  NTRU lattices.
- SLH-DSA (formerly SPHINCS+): A stateless hash-based signature scheme.

For hardware implementations (RTL), Kyber (ML-KEM) and Dilithium (ML-DSA)
are the most practical starting points due to their efficiency and reliance
on similar mathematical foundations (Lattice-based cryptography using 
polynomial rings over finite fields).

3. KEY HARDWARE DESIGN CHALLENGES
---------------------------------
To achieve high-throughput and low-area PQC hardware, we must design 
efficient modules for:

- Number Theoretic Transform (NTT): Fast polynomial multiplication in 
  Z_q[X]/(X^256 + 1) with q = 3329 (for Kyber) or q = 8380417 (for Dilithium).
  We need a configurable NTT butterfly unit (Cooley-Tukey or Gentleman-Sande).
- Symmetric Keccak Core: SHA3-256, SHA3-512, SHAKE-128, and SHAKE-256 are heavily
  used for matrix/vector generation and hashing. A high-throughput pipelined
  Keccak_f1600 permutation engine is crucial.
- Modular Reduction: Implementing fast modular reductions (Montgomery/Barrett)
  to avoid division operations in hardware.
- Memory/SRAM Interfacing: Storing vector/matrix coefficients efficiently to
  minimize read/write conflicts during NTT butterfly computation.


4. NEXT STEPS FOR IMPLEMENTATION
---------------------------------
1. Start with the Keccak (SHA3 / SHAKE) engine as it is used across all lattice PQC.
2. Develop the NTT core, which is the performance bottleneck.
3. Integrate these components into ML-KEM (Kyber) and ML-DSA (Dilithium) pipelines.
